# Job market facts, with sources

Every number in a post comes from this file, quoted as the source states it, with the source named in the caption.
**Status** decides use: `confirmed` may be used; `confirm first` may not be used until Thomas checks it and
changes the status.

## 1. Referred candidates get hired far more often than people applying online. Status: confirmed

- Lever's data on more than 4 million candidates, tens of thousands of hires and about 1,000 employers
  (August 2015 to July 2016) found these hire rates:
  - employee referrals: **1 in 16** hired
  - agency candidates: 1 in 22
  - candidates sourced by recruiters: 1 in 72
  - people applying through a careers site or job posting: **1 in 152**
  - overall: about 1 in 100
- Source: SHRM, "Lever Study Shows 1 in 100 Candidates Is Hired", Roy Maurer, Oct 27, 2016.
  https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/lever-study-shows-1-100-candidates-hired
- Wording: "about ten times" or "almost ten times" is fine (152 / 16 = 9.5). Name the year: it's 2016 data.

## 2. Most people got their job through someone they know. Status: confirmed (used on sidedoorjobs.app)

- **54%** of US workers say they got their job through a connection.
- Source: MyPerfectResume survey of 1,000 US workers, 2025, reported by HR Dive.

## 3. A quarter of external hires come from referrals. Status: confirm first (the year)

- **25%** of external hires come from employee referrals.
- Source: CareerXroads, annual Source of Hire report (employer data). Used on sidedoorjobs.app; find the edition
  year before using it in a post.

## 4. How many people apply for one job. Status: confirm first (primary report)

- About **244 applications per job** on average in 2025, roughly double 2022's 116, per Greenhouse's Hire
  Standard report (March 2026, over 640 million applications).
- Only seen in secondary write-ups so far (e.g. noon.ai, The Interview Guys). Open Greenhouse's own report before
  using it. Don't use the "250 applicants per job" figure: it traces to a 2015 Glassdoor blog post.

## 5. What LinkedIn Premium costs. Status: confirmed, with this wording only

- **Use:** "LinkedIn Premium can cost $50+ a month, depending on the plan." Also fine: "LinkedIn Premium: often
  $50+ a month." In a caption, add "(prices vary by plan and person)".
- **Don't use:** a flat "LinkedIn Premium costs $50+ a month". The Career plan, the one aimed at job seekers, is
  listed below $50 in some reports.
- Basis: Thomas's research (2026-10-25): prices vary from person to person and are usually above $50 a month.
  Reported US list prices: Career $39.99 and Business $69.99 a month (TemperStack, March 2026).
- Comparisons with Sidedoor's prices (`knowledge/sidedoor.md`) only in Sidedoor's own voice (caption brand line or
  the closing slide), e.g. "Sidedoor starts free; Pro is $12.99 a month."
- Never more than the price and what LinkedIn itself says Premium includes; no logo.
